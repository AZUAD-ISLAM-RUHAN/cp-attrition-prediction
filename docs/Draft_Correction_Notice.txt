DRAFT CORRECTION NOTICE (for the Editorial Desk's consideration; wording entirely at the Desk's discretion)
=======================================================================================================

Correction to: A. I. Ruhan, G. M. Naeem, R. I. Rafi, S. A. Mim, N. B. Opi, D. F. Chowdhury, and M. R. K. Sadi,
"Predicting Student Attrition in Competitive Programming: A Large-Scale Study Integrating Survey Insights and
Global Behavioral Logs," AIUB Journal of Science and Engineering, vol. 25, no. 2, pp. 231-243, 2026,
doi: 10.53799/vka5w161.

Following an editorial inquiry, the authors re-examined the article against its raw data and code, which they
have deposited. The following corrections have been made.

1. Behavioral comparisons. The descriptive results on Codeforces behavior (abstract, Section IV, Figs. 4-8,
   conclusion) had been computed on the unmatched cohort of the first submission. They are now computed on the
   rating-matched cohort, after excluding accounts with incomplete retrieved submission histories. The reported
   83.7% fall in contest participation, the 15.6% rise in struggle time and the editorial-dependency
   interpretation are withdrawn. Matched users who withdrew show lower contest and practice frequency and
   slightly lower topic-level accuracy (small effects).
2. Feature definitions and leakage audit. The definitions of the Activity Trend Ratio, the monthly rates and
   post-drop recovery now match their implementation. A single rule removes all window-dependent features. On
   that basis, behavioral classifiers reach a cross-validated ROC-AUC of 0.622, above chance but modest, and
   results are reported against trivial baselines.
3. Survey analysis. Two survey item pairings (Q11/Q33, Q27/Q36) compared non-equivalent items and are no longer
   used for between-group tests. No survey difference survives correction for multiple comparisons. The survey
   classifier, the Early Warning System and the Skill-Application Paradox as a finding are withdrawn; the
   latter is retained as a hypothesis.
4. Ethics statement. The application to the Metropolitan University Research Ethics Committee was considered on 24 February 2026 and approved verbally; the approval letter REC/MU/2026/007 was issued on 17 March 2026. 84 of the 96 survey responses were collected before the letter. (Superseded: the revised paper of November 2026 no longer includes the survey; see the claim table submitted to the journal.)
5. References and metadata. References [5], [9] and [10] are corrected, and four methods references are added.
   The Crossref deposit is updated to the reference list of the published article.

The authors apologise for these errors.
